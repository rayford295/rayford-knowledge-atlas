## Summary

This conference paper defines Responsible GeoAI as a place-aware framework for designing, evaluating, and governing GeoAI systems in real-world settings. It argues that responsible practice must address spatial context, human vulnerability, and social consequences rather than treating model accuracy as the whole problem.

## Method Snapshot

The paper introduces the STEP framework: Security, Trustworthiness, Equity, and Philanthropy. These four dimensions organize questions about who can use GeoAI-derived spatial intelligence, when outputs can support decisions, where benefits and harms are distributed, and how GeoAI can serve public good.

## Conference Context

This paper was presented at GeoAI 2026, the 1st International Conference on Geospatial Artificial Intelligence, held in Ghent, Belgium. It connects the GEAR Lab's Responsible GeoAI line to the conference theme of bridging AI and geography for science and society.

## Impact

The paper adds a conceptual anchor for the atlas's Responsible GeoAI thread. It turns responsibility from a general ethical label into a set of operational questions that can guide GeoAI model design, evaluation, deployment, and future duty-based governance.
