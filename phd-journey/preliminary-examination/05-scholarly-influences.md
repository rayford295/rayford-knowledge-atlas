# 05 — Scholarly Influences

Three researchers outside my home department have shaped how I think about cross-view GeoAI and disaster informatics. I read them not only for methods but for how they frame the relationship between data, place, and responsibility.

## Dr. Yingjie Hu (University at Buffalo)

A leading figure in GeoAI in recent years. His work on geographic information extraction, place-based reasoning, and the integration of language and spatial data informs my conviction that disaster assessment is not only a vision problem but a problem of grounding meaning in place. The emphasis on making geographic AI legible and useful, rather than merely accurate, runs parallel to my own responsibility theme.

## Dr. Hao Li (National University of Singapore)

Trained in Germany and now at NUS, his research on geospatial foundation models and the use of volunteered and street-level imagery for mapping is directly relevant to my cross-view pipeline. His trajectory shows how street-level and overhead data can be combined into general-purpose geospatial reasoning, which is the technical lineage my work sits within.

## Dr. Zihui Ma (University of Maryland)

His work connecting social media signals and disaster events informs the human and communication side of my framework. Where my imagery-based assessment sees the physical damage, this line of work is a reminder that disaster perception also lives in what people report and how information reaches them, which matters for the delivery-and-feedback layer.

## Why these three

Together they cover the three edges my dissertation tries to connect: place-grounded reasoning, cross-view and foundation-model methods, and the human and communicative dimension of disaster. Reading across them is part of why I treat disaster GeoAI as a system that must reach people, not only as a model that must score well.

> Note: affiliations and descriptions reflect my understanding at the time of writing and may need updating as these researchers move and publish.
